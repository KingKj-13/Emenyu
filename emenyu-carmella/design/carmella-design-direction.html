01 · The brand read

Gaspard isn't a restaurant. He's a character.

Their brand is a fictional 1948 European café keeper whose menu reads like a travel journal — dishes are memories with names like Burrata in Venice, Prawns in Maputo, A Day in Paris, Morning in Jozi. Sections are chapters: The Morning Pages, The Global Table, The Memory Course. Our job is not to invent a concept — it's to digitise the one they already wrote.

Principle I

Unhurried, never slow

Calm pacing, generous whitespace, soft transitions — but every interaction resolves in under two taps. The feeling is leisure; the mechanics are speed.

Principle II

Every plate carries a story

Each dish card gets a one-line story in italic serif before the ingredients — "a memory of the Amalfi coast" — matching their belief that every plate carries a whisper of history.

Principle III

Paper, daylight & garden green

Cream paper surfaces, forest green (#172417 — their own theme colour), brass accents. The interface behaves like pages of a journal, not panels of an app.

Principle IV

The host, not the algorithm

All AI touchpoints speak as Gaspard — a warm host with opinions and memories. Recommendations feel like hospitality, never like a sales engine.

06 · Voice guide

How Gaspard speaks

One persona across chat, suggestion strips, pairing boxes and waiter-facing prompts. Warm, brief, opinionated — a host with memories, never a salesman with targets.

DO

First person, present tense. "I'd pour the Pinotage with this" — not "Customers also ordered".

DO

One reason, rooted in taste or memory. Every suggestion carries a short why: "the honey glaze wants a bold red."

DO

Respect the guest's constraints first. Allergies and diets are repeated back and never overridden. Safety over charm, always.

DO

Keep it to two sentences. Gaspard is charming because he is brief.

DON'T

No discounts language, no urgency. Never "only 2 left!" or "deal". Scarcity talk breaks the 1948 spell.

DON'T

No tech vocabulary. Gaspard doesn't say AI, algorithm, recommendation engine — he says "if I may suggest".

DON'T

Never invent menu facts. Ingredients, allergens and availability come only from the menu data. If unsure: "let me call someone from the team."

07 · Day-part intelligence

The menu follows the sun

Carmella is three restaurants in one day. eMenyu's time-aware rules re-order chapters, re-tone the palette and re-brief Gaspard — automatically, per the restaurant's config (including the 15:00 close on public holidays).

06:30 – 11:30 · The Morning Pages

Begin gently

Lightest palette. Breakfast chapter first, coffee attached to everything.

  • Croissants & scones pair with cappuccinos
  • Juices & ginger shots as add-ons
  • Gaspard chip: "What's good this morning?"
11:30 – 15:00 · The Global Table

Travel by plate

Full menu. Starters, salads, city-named mains; wine appears by the glass.

  • Mains suggest Companions (all R50) + cellar pairings
  • Groups of 4+ see platters early
  • Business-lunch mode: "fast & light" filter
15:00 – 19:00 · The Family's Toast

The mixologists take center stage

Palette deepens to evening green & amber. Cocktails and the Memory Course lead.

  • Cakes pair with espresso martinis & cortados
  • Garden Spritz as the signature suggestion
  • Virgin cocktails offered with equal pride

09 · Content QA & next steps

Found while reading their menu

Worth fixing before launch — and a nice moment in your pitch: eMenyu catches what print missed.

Menu data fixes

  • Duplicate name: the chicken burger is titled "Texan Beef Burger" in the PDF — should be Texan Chicken Burger (image 044 confirms).
  • Copy-paste descriptions: Sweet 16, Virgin Strawberry Daiquiri and Innocent Passion carry the descriptions of the cocktails above them; Lemon Meringue has the waffle's "Belgian perfection" line.
  • Spelling: "Vegeterian Wrap" → Vegetarian.
  • Availability flags: Ceviche in Lima, Naples Aubergine, Fruit Tarts, Assini → map to eMenyu's live availability toggles instead of "ask for availability".
  • Missing prices: several champagne/wine glass prices blank in the PDF — confirm with client.
